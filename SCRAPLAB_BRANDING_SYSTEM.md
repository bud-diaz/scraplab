# SCRAPLAB BRANDING SYSTEM
Version: 1.0
Status: Locked Foundation Brand Direction

---

# Brand Core

## Brand Name
ScrapLab

---

## Brand Positioning
ScrapLab is a modern family creativity platform that transforms everyday household materials into imaginative, kid-friendly build experiences.

It is not merely a craft app.

ScrapLab exists at the intersection of:

- creative problem-solving
- playful family engagement
- structured imagination
- household resourcefulness
- safe guided experimentation

Core promise:

**Build cool things with what you already have.**

---

# Brand Thesis

ScrapLab should feel like:

- a modern maker workshop
- a family creativity operating system
- a smart build assistant
- a playful invention lab

It should NOT feel like:

- a chaotic Pinterest craft blog
- a discount hobby store
- a preschool classroom handout
- a generic AI SaaS dashboard
- a teacher-made worksheet platform
- a STEM subscription box clone

---

# Emotional Brand Narrative

Primary emotional lane:

**"Kid who loves hanging with grandpa who's a retired contractor who frequented Home Depot."**

Interpretation:

- hands-on creativity
- practical ingenuity
- building over consuming
- warmth
- workshop nostalgia
- safe experimentation
- making something from almost nothing

This emotional framing should influence all design and product decisions.

---

# Brand Personality

ScrapLab is:

- inventive
- warm
- capable
- trustworthy
- playful
- resourceful
- imaginative
- premium-friendly
- hands-on
- encouraging

ScrapLab is NOT:

- hyper-corporate
- sterile
- chaotic
- childish
- cluttered
- bargain-bin crafty
- overly educational
- aggressively "startup"

---

# Brand Voice

Tone should feel:

- confident
- warm
- concise
- lightly playful
- encouraging
- smart without sounding academic

Examples:

GOOD:
"Nice. You’ve got enough to build a rocket."

GOOD:
"Only have cardboard and tape? Challenge accepted."

GOOD:
"This one’s a quick win."

BAD:
"Congratulations learner! Let's begin your STEM creativity journey!"

BAD:
"Leverage household inventory for optimized project generation."

# Color Usage Rules

Tan should be dominant.

Blue should guide interaction.

Orange should be used sparingly for energy moments.

Brown should ground the experience.

Avoid excessive rainbow color usage.

Do NOT default into generic SaaS navy/white aesthetics.

# Logo Direction

## Locked Direction
Abstract modular construction mark.

NOT mascot-based.

Logo should feel:

- premium
- scalable
- construction-inspired
- modular
- intelligent
- tactile

Potential directions:

### Option A: Modular "S"
An abstract S built from geometric construction pieces.

Examples:
- rectangles
- connectors
- folded panels
- modular blocks

Best for:
software identity
favicon
app icon
product branding

---

### Option B: Folded Construction Mark
An abstract folded shape suggesting:

- cardboard
- assembly
- invention
- imagination

May subtly imply:
- S
- cube
- spark
- box

---

## Explicit Logo Avoidances

Do NOT create:

- cartoon mascots
- literal cardboard robots
- junk collages
- fake rustic signage
- construction company logos
- hardware store parody branding

# UI Design Principles

The product must feel:

- fast
- obvious
- tactile
- low-friction
- premium
- safe
- parent-trustworthy

Users should feel:

"I can do this."

Not:

"This app is making this harder."

Avoid:

- Pinterest clutter
- scrapbook chaos
- faux classroom aesthetic
- toy-store overload
- overwhelming dashboards
- enterprise SaaS spacing
- overly gamified chaos

not
